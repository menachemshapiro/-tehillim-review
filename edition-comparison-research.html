Evidence from a comparison of the printed text of Tehillim Ohel Yosef Yitzchak.

Purpose and scope

This comparison asks whether the 2012 Kehot edition preserves the vocalized Hebrew text of the earlier printing. It does not attempt to determine which edition is more accurate or whether any particular change was intentional.

Three categories must be distinguished: differences in glyph design, differences in cantillation signs, and changes in the actual vocalization. The last category provides the strongest evidence that the editions differ in textual content, rather than merely in the appearance of the type.

3. Conclusion

The addition and omission of dageshim in the bet, together with the differing vowel configuration at Psalm 32:6, provide evidence of changes beyond the graphical appearance of the printed text. The cantillation comparisons further demonstrate differences, although some recurring changes may reflect alternative conventions for rendering the same sign. The conclusion does not depend on establishing which edition is correct, or on attributing the changes to any particular editorial process.

Source and method

Paired crops are taken from the original old-print scan and new-edition image in the Tehillim review project. The selected examples are based on the manual review export, including representative supplementary confirmed cases. The precise bibliographic details of the earlier printing and the exact classifications of uncertain accents should be verified before publication. The reconstruction is not used as photographic evidence.
